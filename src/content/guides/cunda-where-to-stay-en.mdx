The search for accommodation in Cunda usually starts with the same question: a stone house, a pension, or a boutique hotel? The island's lodging scene is a direct reflection of [its century-old history](/en/guide/cunda-history) — restored Greek houses, family-run pensions, and boutique hotels in historic buildings all share the same stone lanes. This guide explains who each option suits best and how to choose according to your budget.

## Stone House and Greek House Hotels

The two-story stone houses that have become Cunda's signature were built by the Greek community that lived on the island before the population exchange. Some of these buildings have been restored as hotels today; their thick stone walls stay cool in summer and warm in winter, and every room has a character of its own. Staying in a building with protected historic status means becoming part of the island's story for a night — [our hotel, Cunda Milos](/en/rooms), operates in exactly such a Greek house, built in 1907.

When choosing a stone house hotel, we suggest checking three things: whether the building is genuinely historic (newly built "stone-look" properties exist too), whether the rooms preserve the original architecture, and the walking distance to the market square.

## Pensions: Intimate and Economical

The family-run pensions of Cunda and Ayvalık are the area's most affordable accommodation. Sharing a courtyard with your hosts, waking to breakfasts with homemade jams — these small guesthouses are budget-friendly, especially for longer stays and off-season visits. In return, adjust your expectations accordingly: room comfort and service standards are simpler than in hotels.

## Boutique Hotels: The Best of Both Worlds

Cunda's boutique hotels combine the intimacy of a pension with the comfort of a hotel. Properties with only a handful of rooms can give every guest personal attention; breakfast is prepared with local produce, and recommendations come from islanders rather than guidebooks. As a six-room stone house hotel, [the Cunda Milos experience](/en/about) is built on exactly this balance: the atmosphere of a historic Greek house, modern room comfort, and personalized service.

## Tips for an Affordable Stay

An affordable stay in Cunda is entirely possible — provided you pick your timing:

- **Consider the off-season.** From October to May, prices sit well below summer rates, and the island is at its calmest and most photogenic.
- **Prefer weeknights.** Friday and Saturday nights see peak demand; Sunday–Thursday stays are cheaper at most properties.
- **Book early.** Boutique hotels with few rooms fill up months ahead for summer weekends; booking early secures both a room and a better rate.
- **Ask about longer stays.** Many properties offer special rates for three nights or more — [just ask us on WhatsApp](/en/contact).

## Cunda, Ayvalık Town Center, or Sarımsaklı?

Three areas, three different holidays. **Cunda** is for those who love spending time on the island itself, with its historic stone streets, fishermen's restaurants, and calm atmosphere; most of the [places worth visiting](/en/guide/cunda-places-to-visit) are reachable on foot. **Ayvalık town center** suits travelers seeking transport convenience, close to the bus terminal and bazaar. **Sarımsaklı**, with its long sandy beach, is the address for a beach-focused holiday — and [Cunda's coves](/en/guide/cunda-beaches) are a short drive away.
